# Hossein Akhlaghpour — Application Answers 2026

## Startup / Early-Stage Experience

### Prompt
What experience do you have working at startups or early-stage companies? Please share as much detail as possible about the companies (e.g., size, industry/domain, stage, and your role). If you haven’t worked at a startup before, what interests you about working in this kind of environment?

### Draft Answer
I have worked in startup environments at very different stages, from very early technical foundations to scaling teams and products.

One of the most formative experiences was at Genapsys, a genomics / DNA-sequencing startup. I joined very early, when the company was still small — roughly five or six people — and I was the first software developer. I led the software side for almost four years as the company grew significantly, eventually reaching close to 200 people and raising roughly $70M. My work covered the core software platform around sequencing workflows, including embedded systems, backend services, cloud infrastructure, data processing, and algorithmic work for signal processing, denoising, and base-calling. That experience taught me what it feels like to build software when the product, team, and company are all evolving at the same time.

I also started Vitachain, a digital-health startup focused on using machine learning and health data to build personalized recommendations and prediction systems. The company did not become commercially successful, but I was able to build real technical solutions, including ML models using wearable and health data, recommendation systems, and prototypes around digital-health workflows. That experience gave me a very practical understanding of startup ambiguity: making product decisions with incomplete information, building quickly, talking to users, and learning from what does not work.

I like early-stage environments because they reward ownership. You cannot stay inside a narrow job description. You need to understand the product, the customer, the technical constraints, the business model, and the team’s current reality. That is also why I am interested in applied AI and forward-deployed AI roles: the work is not just building models, but figuring out what problem is worth solving, how to turn messy real-world data into usable context, and how to ship something that actually changes a workflow.

## Tell Me About Yourself — AI Architect

### Prompt
Tell me about yourself and your career journey so far. Focus on what uniquely positions you for an AI Architect role and why you are moving on from Intapp.

### Short Interview Answer
My career has been a combination of software architecture, machine learning, and enterprise AI.

Earlier in my career, I worked on large-scale software and data systems at companies like eBay and Velti, and then joined Genapsys very early as the first software developer. I led the software side there as the company grew significantly, which gave me a lot of experience building systems from zero and scaling them in an ambiguous startup environment.

Later, I moved deeper into machine learning. At AutoGrid, I worked on production ML systems for smart-grid optimization, including systems used to control thousands of batteries. At Intapp, my focus shifted to enterprise NLP and GenAI for legal, CRM, and private-capital workflows.

What makes me a strong fit for AI Architect roles is that I understand the full stack of enterprise AI: models, data, APIs, permissions, workflows, evaluation, guardrails, and production reliability. In my view, enterprise AI is not just a model problem. The real challenge is turning messy client data, documents, schemas, and workflows into context that AI systems can use safely and reliably.

At Intapp, I worked on natural-language interfaces over customized enterprise schemas, secure translation of user intent into API calls, information extraction, narrative generation, and production patterns for validation and governance.

My role was impacted by restructuring, so I’m now focusing on roles that are more directly aligned with where I want to grow: AI architecture, enterprise GenAI, context engineering, RAG, and production AI systems.
